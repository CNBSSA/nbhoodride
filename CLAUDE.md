## What this product is

**PGRide** — ride-sharing for Prince George's County, MD (USA): riders, driver-partners, Stripe/cash fares, wallet ledger, driver equity program, super-admin ops. Stack: Node.js/TypeScript, React, PostgreSQL, Railway (`nbhoodride-production.up.railway.app`).

## Workflow

Develop on `develop`; promote to `main` only with Festus's explicit approval after testing. Planning → change-impact audit → implement → test → post-implementation audit.

**Promotion timing (standing instruction, Festus 2026-09-01):** promotions to `main` are batched to the daily quiet window (~03:30 ET / 07:30 UTC) so deploy restarts never blip riders during riding hours. Merge reviewed work to `develop` any time; it ships to `main` in the next window. Exception: urgent rider-blocking fixes promote immediately.


**Nothing built is removed (company policy, Festus 2026-09-16):** a feature, menu, screen or button that has shipped stays. New work adds alongside what exists — a new button, a new field, a new door — and may make an existing feature work better, but it never removes, hides or renames what a rider, driver, desk or admin already uses. A change that would remove or replace something shipped needs Festus's explicit approval in the request, named as a removal, before it is built. Every `data-testid="button-…"` in `client/src` is covered by the every-button audit; a button that disappears from the codebase is a removal and is treated as one, and CI enforces it: `npm run check:buttons-kept` (`scripts/e2e/shipped-buttons.mjs`) fails when any button in the register `scripts/e2e/shipped-buttons.json` is gone from the source without an entry under `retired` carrying reason, approver and date. New buttons are registered with `node scripts/e2e/shipped-buttons.mjs --update`.

**Rider Promise Review (Festus 2026-09-07):** "reliable" means a rider who books a ride gets picked up, on time, at the quoted price — every time. The numbers are defined in `shared/riderPromise.ts` (rides delivered, strandings, fare accuracy, late pickups, plus what is unclaimed in the next 24h) and computed in `server/riderPromiseReview.ts`. The server posts it to the ops Telegram chat once a day at 4:00 AM Eastern; it is separate from, and named differently to, the GitHub "Daily Reliability Report" (code health). Admin: `GET/POST /api/admin/analytics/rider-promise-review`.

**Proactive reliability (Festus 2026-09-09):** "reliable" also means every feature, menu and button works, and the operator hears about trouble before the rider does. `server/rideRiskWatch.ts` (rules in `shared/rideRisk.ts`) pages the ops Telegram when a scheduled ride is still unclaimed at T-2h and T-15m, and once at T-10 when the driver has no fresh position or is more than 4 miles from the pickup (stamps o120/o15/o10 in `rides.reminder_stamps`; admin `POST /api/admin/analytics/ride-risk-sweep`). Every rider alert, including app crashes from the error boundary (`client_crash`), is also written to `reliability_events`, and the Rider Promise Review reports "App health" and "Paged ahead" lines from it. Production is watched two ways: `server/dependencyWatch.ts` checks the database and Stripe every 10 minutes from inside the server and pages on down/recovered (`GET /health/deps`, admin `POST /api/admin/analytics/dependency-check`); `.github/workflows/production-watch.yml` probes from outside every 10 minutes (`npm run watch:production`) and pages Telegram on a change when the repo secrets `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` / `TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID` are set. Every pull request also runs the every-button audit (`npm run test:buttons`, `scripts/e2e/button-audit.mjs`): as visitor, rider, driver and admin it opens every screen and presses every safe button, failing on a crash, blank screen, red toast or 5xx. Every `data-testid="button-…"` in `client/src` must be pressed by it or listed in `scripts/e2e/button-audit-baseline.json` with a reason, so no new button ships untested.

**Commercial riders (Festus 2026-09-10, plan: "Commercial Riders" artifact):** organizations (a dialysis center, an office, a restaurant) book rides and deliveries for other people and are billed; the business places every order, the person served never holds an account. Built as a modular monolith in vertical slices behind `COMMERCIAL_ENABLED` (off in production until a slice is proven). One spine: every job is a row in `rides` (`payment_method = 'invoice'`, `ride_type = 'commercial'`, passenger by name/phone) plus a `commercial_jobs` row; one door: `server/commercial/` and routes under `/api/admin/organizations/*` and `/api/org/*`, every member route through `requireMember` scoping to the organization; pure rules and statement math in `shared/commercial.ts`. Paging for a commercial job names the account and job number, never the passenger. Slice 1 (organizations, book for a client, monthly statement) shipped first; slices 2–6 (portal, standing orders, badges, weekly billing, deliveries) follow in order. Slice 2: the requester portal at `/org` (`client/src/pages/portal/PortalPage.tsx`) is desktop first and installable from the browser (manifest orientation any, a wide screenshot, `usePwaInstallPrompt`): the next 48 hours as a board with a map, all jobs with cancel, the statement, and people (owners); `N` opens the booking form, `Ctrl+Enter` books. Member routes added: `GET /api/org/:orgId`, `POST/DELETE /api/org/:orgId/members` (owners), `POST /api/org/:orgId/jobs/:jobId/cancel` (bookers; rider ladder until slice 3; a job on the road cannot be cancelled from the desk). The harness seeds a standing organization (`e2e-org`, rider as owner) so the every-button audit (requester role) and the layout audit's desk-and-phone pass can open the portal. Slice 3: an organization's own terms replace the rider ladder for its jobs (`shared/commercialTerms.ts`, stored in `organizations.terms`, admin `PATCH /api/admin/organizations/:id { terms }`, shown to the desk as `termsText` on `GET /api/org/:orgId`): free cancel window, late-cancel fee, no-show fee, free waiting minutes and per-minute rate, will-call lead. Standing orders (`commercial_standing_orders`, `server/commercial/standingOrders.ts`) book jobs a week ahead, one per (order, Eastern service date, leg) enforced by a unique index, from the minute sweep every 5 minutes and on create/resume; `POST /api/org/:orgId/jobs/:jobId/return` books a will-call return with pickup and destination swapped at least the organization's lead out. Waiting at the door and no-shows are priced from the terms onto the job at completion and no-show (`server/commercial/waiting.ts`), so a commercial no-show charges nobody's card. Slice 4: driver badges (`shared/driverBadges.ts`, `driver_profiles.badges`) decide who may take commercial work — the badge follows what the job IS (`kindOfJob` in `shared/commercial.ts`: a parcel is a job with a parcel on it, everything else carries a person) — a parcel needs `delivery` whoever sends it, a person on a medical account's job needs `medical`, a person on a business or food account's job is an ordinary ride and needs none (until 2026-09-16 every business job, rides included, went only to parcel-cleared drivers). The desk has two doors, **Book a ride** (`N`) for every account and **Send a parcel** (`P`) for business and food accounts; the parcel drawer was built with slice 6 and not mounted by any page until 2026-09-16. The claim board hides work a driver is not cleared for (`getOpenScheduledRides(counties, badges)`) and the claim and accept routes refuse it with the badge named (`server/commercial/badges.ts`); the operator grants badges per driver in the admin drivers panel (`PUT /api/admin/drivers/:userId/badges`), and a driver sees their own at `GET /api/driver/badges`. On a claim the passenger, who holds no account, is texted the driver's first name and a guardian tracking link, and the message is logged verbatim so it is auditable when Twilio is unreachable. `POST /api/driver/rides/:rideId/proof` records who received the passenger; it shows on the desk's job row and in the statement's "Received by" column. Slice 5: weekly billing (`shared/billingCycle.ts`, `commercial_statements`, `server/commercial/billing.ts`). A billing week runs Monday to Monday Eastern and is named by its Monday's date; last week is issued and debited the following Monday at 9 AM Eastern from the minute sweep, claimed once per week through `claimWebhookEvent` so restarts cannot double-charge. Issuing stamps every job with `statement_id` and `billed_status`, and a unique index on (organization, week) makes re-issuing a no-op — a job is billed exactly once, and a failed collection never releases it. Collection is an off-session Stripe PaymentIntent against the organization's bank account (preferred) or card, idempotency-keyed by statement; a bank debit that reports `processing` stays `charging` until it settles. Failures are recorded on the statement with their reason and paged to ops. Accounts on `net_terms` are issued statements and never charged automatically. The desk attaches a bank account under Billing in the portal (`POST /api/org/:orgId/billing/setup-intent` then `.../payment-method`); the payment method id is never sent to the browser. Admin: `POST /api/admin/organizations/:id/statements`, `POST /api/admin/commercial-statements/:id/charge`, `POST /api/admin/analytics/weekly-billing`. Slice 6: deliveries (`shared/deliveries.ts`, `server/commercial/deliveries.ts`) are commercial jobs with no passenger — parcel size, a pickup contact, a drop contact and a window, on the same spine (the ride's "passenger" is who receives the parcel, so the driver's card names them). They carry their own tariff ($9 covers the first 3 miles, then $1.60 a mile) through `bookJob`'s `fareOverride`; the driver still keeps 85%. Only a business or food account may book one (`POST /api/org/:orgId/deliveries`), only a driver with the `delivery` badge sees or claims it, and the handover is the same `POST /api/driver/rides/:rideId/proof` with a photo. The desk books it in a parcel-shaped drawer and sees the parcel line on every job row; the claim board carries `commercialKind` so a driver's card can say what the work is.

**The business door (Festus 2026-09-16):** a desk user signs in at `/org/login` (`client/src/pages/portal/BusinessLogin.tsx`) — same account, password, endpoint and limits as the rider sign-in, but it lands in the portal and is remembered on that device (`client/src/lib/businessHome.ts`; the portal's own "rider app" links clear it). Signed out, `/org` shows the business sign-in and comes back to the same organization afterwards; before this it showed "not found". An owner adding an email with no account no longer gets "they need to sign up first": an invitation is created (`organization_invitations`, `server/commercial/invitations.ts`, rules in `shared/invitations.ts`), emailed when email is configured and always shown to the desk as a link to send, and the invitee sets name, phone and password on `/org/join/:token` and lands in the desk as a member, approved on acceptance because the organization's owner vouched for them. Links are single use, expire in 7 days, and only their hash is stored. The rider sign-in, signup and Profile's "Open organization portal" are unchanged.

**Proof of delivery (Festus 2026-09-17, "a photo only when nobody signs"):** how a parcel changes hands is a field on the job (`commercial_jobs.handover`: `person` | `reception` | `unattended`, chosen in the parcel drawer; rules in `shared/deliveries.ts`), and it decides the proof. A person or reception signs by name; at the door the photo is the signature. The driver's active card shows the handover line, parcel, window and drop note for a parcel (`/api/driver/active-rides` carries `delivery`), and Complete opens the handover sheet (`client/src/components/DeliveryProofSheet.tsx`) before completing; the server refuses completing a parcel without its proof (409 `needsProof`) and accepts only photos uploaded through PG Ride's own object store. No signal at the door does not strand the driver: the photo is kept on the phone (`client/src/lib/proofPhoto.ts`), the job completes with "photo pending", and the photo follows for 24 hours. The driver's position is recorded on the proof and a handover more than 150 m from the drop address is flagged to the desk, never blocked. Members of the organization that booked the job may open the proof photo; the desk's job row reads "received by … · photo".

**Recipient approval (Festus 2026-09-17, "the recipient has to approve"):** the shop always pays PG Ride and collects the delivery fee from its customer itself; what it may ask for is the customer's yes before the parcel is sent. A business or food desk books a parcel with `askRecipient: true` (the parcel form's "Ask the recipient to approve the delivery fee first"; the owner's default under Billing, `organizations.ask_recipient_by_default`). The job is HELD — `commercial_jobs.recipient_approval` in (`awaiting`, `declined`) — so the claim board, the scheduled-ride sweep, the ride-risk watch and the Rider Promise Review skip it, the claim itself refuses it, and no driver is asked, until the recipient approves from the link they are texted (`/approve/:token`, `client/src/pages/RecipientApprovalPage.tsx`; rules and words in `shared/recipientApproval.ts`, server in `server/commercial/recipientApproval.ts`) or the desk sends it anyway. No money moves: the fee stays on the shop's statement as for any delivery, and nothing about the goods is shown. The recipient can decline (the shop is told); the desk can send anyway, copy or re-text the link. The minute sweep nudges the recipient once after 15 minutes, asks the shop once the parcel is ready, and cancels an unanswered job when its window closes; every early end of a commercial ride closes an open link. Every text is logged verbatim. Admin: `POST /api/admin/analytics/recipient-approval-sweep`.

**The recipient book (Festus 2026-09-16, "the house is the house"):** a delivery account's saved recipients (`organization_recipients`, rules in `shared/recipients.ts`, server in `server/commercial/recipients.ts`, routes `GET/POST/DELETE /api/org/:orgId/recipients` for bookers) — name, phone, address, handover and note. A recipient is saved from the booking itself ("Remember this recipient", on by default, `rememberRecipient: true` on `POST /api/org/:orgId/deliveries`); the same phone is one entry however often it is saved. The parcel drawer offers "Deliver to a saved recipient", starts the pickup at the organization's own address, and every parcel job row and every book entry has "Send again"/"Send", which opens the drawer filled in (`parcel` on the job row carries what was sent and to whom). Edits never rewrite past jobs (each keeps its own snapshot) and removing a recipient archives it; the Recipients tab shows the book.

**Standing deliveries (slice 4 of the delivery plan, 2026-09-17):** a standing order may be a parcel (`commercial_standing_orders.kind = 'delivery'` with parcel size, handover, pickup and drop contacts and a window): the same recipient on the same days, booked a week ahead by the same sweep and the same unique (order, service date, leg) index, each occurrence through `bookDelivery` so it carries its tariff, handover and proof rules. No return leg. Account-pays only in this version — a recurring recipient-pays job would text the recipient every day — and only a business or food account may create one. The desk's standing-order form offers "A parcel" to delivery accounts.

**After the handover (standard practice, decided 2026-09-17):** when a parcel is completed the receiver is texted "delivered at <time>, received by … / left at the door" with a link to `/delivered/:token` (`client/src/pages/DeliveredPage.tsx`, `server/commercial/delivered.ts`; the token lives on the job as `proof_share_token`), which shows the shop, when, the handover and the photo — never the goods. Proof photos are kept 90 days and the record forever: an hourly sweep (`retireOldProofPhotos`, admin `POST /api/admin/analytics/retire-proof-photos`) deletes the stored photo and marks the proof `photoRetired`; the desk's row and the receiver's page say so. Revoking approval or suspending an account ends every session it holds at once (`adminUpdateUser`).

**Who gets what (rates audit, 2026-09-18):** the split is `shared/payoutPolicy.ts`: 85% of the fare to the driver, 15% to PG Ride, all of the tip to the driver, card fees out of PG Ride's share. Which discounts are PG Ride's is decided there too (`driverBasisFor`, used by `completeRide`): a welcome credit or promotion and the weekly-plan rate are PG Ride's, so the driver is paid on the fare before them; a coworker or shared seat is a rate, not a discount, so the driver is paid 85% of each seat as a shared trip is priced everywhere in the industry. A commercial driver is paid at completion whichever door completed the ride (`payCommercialDriverForCompletedRide` in `server/routes.ts`, also on an early end), gets the ordinary cut of the organization's late-cancel fee at once (`payDriverForLateCancel`, 80% driver / 20% community pool, as on a rider's late cancel), and a community bonus is credited to the wallet when it is allocated (`community_bonus`), not only recorded.

**Watchdog on the watchdogs (Festus 2026-09-10):** the daily reliability report is now sent to the ops Telegram as a digest (`shared/reliabilityDigest.ts`, `scripts/reliability-digest.mjs`, last step of `daily-reliability-agent.yml`) as well as commented on the rolling GitHub issue, with a link to the full report; it is inert and green when the Telegram secrets are absent. Every overnight check leaves an hourly heartbeat in `reliability_events` (kind `watch_ran`, `server/watchHeartbeat.ts`): the minute sweep, the dependency watch and the ride-risk watch note themselves from inside the watch (so a manual admin run counts too), and the two GitHub checks mark themselves by adding `?probe=production-watch` / `?probe=daily-reliability` to the health endpoint they already call — no new secret or endpoint, and only known watch names are accepted. The 4:00 AM Rider Promise Review carries an "Overnight checks" line, and a day where every ride promise was kept but a check did not run reads amber, not green. This exists because the production watch once shipped as invalid YAML, never ran for a day, and nothing said so.


**Tips after a card ride (rates audit, 2026-09-18):** a card rider had no way to tip. Now the rating screen offers a tip for the driver after a completed card ride whose fare has settled (`shared/tipPolicy.ts`: presets $2/$3/$5 or $1–$100, once per ride, within 7 days; a ride whose settlement failed, is disputed or was refunded takes none), charged to the card on file as its own payment (`POST /api/rides/:rideId/tip`, `stripeService.chargeTip`) and recorded once under a per-ride lock (`storage.recordRideTipOnce`: the ride's `tip_amount` and `driver_earnings` grow by it, the driver's wallet is credited all of it under `tip`). The ride is claimed (`processed_webhook_events`, provider `ride_tip`) before the card is charged, so two requests racing charge once; the card is charged before anything is written; a tip already on the ledger is refused before the card is touched, and a charge that finds a *different* tip already there is refunded on the spot, in the route and in the webhook, and a refund that fails pages ops. A tip's PaymentIntent carries `type: tip` and the Stripe webhook records it through the same once-only write, never marking the ride paid; a charge that comes back `processing` is told "on its way" and recorded by that webhook. A tip chosen on the rating card is sent with the rating. The every-button audit never presses `button-tip-send-*` because it charges a card.

**Honest figures (rates audit, 2026-09-18):** the admin finances screen keeps "Total revenue" as it always was (the gross: every fare, tip and fee that passed through, drivers' share included) and shows beside it PG Ride's share (`platformShare`, 15% of fares before card fees), how much of that share was collected (`platformShareCollected`: cards that settled and statements that were paid; a cash fare and a statement still owed are not), the drivers' share, and that cancellation fees go to drivers and the community pool. A commercial no-show names the organization's own fee, not the rider ladder's, everywhere it is reported; if that fee could not be written to the job, nobody is billed and nothing is taken from the requester's own wallet, but the driver is still paid the ordinary cut from PG Ride's float and ops are paged to put the account's statement right. A commercial late cancel marks the ride `cancelled_with_fee` like every other cancel that carried one, so the figures count it. The recipient of a parcel is asked to approve what the shop is billed for the delivery (tariff plus the account's facility fee). An account on net terms is never charged by PG Ride, by the weekly run or by hand. A circuit seat is paid by card like every booking.

**Cash is discontinued (Festus 2026-09-18, "cash ride to be discontinued"):** PG Ride no longer takes cash. `shared/paymentMethods.ts` holds the rule and the words: a new ride is paid by card or billed to an organization, nothing else. Booking refuses cash by name, `storage.createRide` refuses it for every booking door, and the one write that cannot go through it (a commercial job, which shares its transaction) carries the same check, and `rides.payment_method` now defaults to `card`, so a row written without one is no longer born a cash ride. **Nothing is removed:** every screen and route that handles a cash ride stays, because rides taken before today still have to be finished, paid, receipted and reported — the driver's Payment Confirmations screen (which now says why nothing new will arrive), the tip a driver enters for one, the wallet fee collection, the "Cash" line on a receipt, the earnings. `confirmCashPayment` now refuses a ride that was not taken in cash, so it can never close a card ride without charging the card; a ride from back then with no payment method recorded still counts as cash (`settlesInCash`), because the column has always allowed nothing and the old default was cash, and its driver must still be able to confirm the money they took. The admin finances screen carries "Cash Rides (discontinued)" with how many are still waiting on a driver to confirm the money, so the tail can be watched out.

**A rider's preferences are never a null (crash fix, 2026-09-20):** a rider tapping Profile met the error screen — `null is not an object (evaluating 'calmRideMode')`. The app-wide language provider asks for `/api/user/ride-preferences` on every page including ones a signed-out visitor opens, and deliberately takes `null` for an answer on a 401 rather than bouncing a visitor to the sign-in page (2026-09-16). Profile reads the same React Query cache entry, and its default was written `const { data = {...} }`, which applies to `undefined` and not to `null` — so a session that died while the app was open turned the Profile tab into a crash. Both readers now go through one hook, `client/src/hooks/useRidePreferences.ts`, which maps whatever came back through `ridePreferencesOrDefaults` (`shared/ridePreferences.ts`): a whole answer, a partial one, nothing, or that `null` all become a rider's preferences, so neither screen is ever handed a null to read. The every-button audit carries the regression: it answers that one request with a 401, opens the rider's Profile and requires a screen (`unauthorised-preferences`, runnable alone with `BUTTON_AUDIT_ONLY=unauthorised-preferences`). It fails on the code that shipped and passes on the fix.

**Nobody waits invisibly (Festus 2026-09-22, "a driver registered but the approval never appeared on my side"):** three things met. A driver's application is a row of its own (`driver_profiles`, approval status pending) and never appears in the pending USERS queue — applying does not set `users.isDriver`, so an applicant still reads "Rider · Active" on their user card, and only the Drivers queue shows they are waiting. Every admin queue was cached until something invalidated it (`staleTime: Infinity`, no refetch on focus), so an operator with the dashboard open saw whatever was true when the page loaded; the pending users, drivers, dashboard and users lists now refresh on a 30-second poll and when the window is focused (`QUEUE_REFRESH` in `client/src/pages/AdminDashboard.tsx`), as the emergency and settlement queues already did. And an application that failed to save told nobody: the successful one pages ops, the failed one only wrote a log line, so an applicant could be told "pending" while the operator had nothing to approve — `POST /api/driver/profile` now pages "🚙 Driver application FAILED" with the reason. Every ops alert is also written to the log verbatim (`[ops-alert]`, `server/telegramOps.ts`), delivered or not, so an alert is auditable when Telegram is unconfigured or down — the same rule the passenger texts already follow. Journey 28 covers all of it.

**A driver from the street to the road (journey 29, 2026-09-22):** every piece of driver onboarding was covered somewhere and the path through them nowhere, so when a driver uploaded his documents, was told they were on file, and the operator's Approve refused him as incomplete, the only person who could find out was Festus with a driver waiting in front of him. `scripts/e2e/29-driver-onboarding.journey.mjs` walks the whole path: sign up, be approved as a person, apply to drive, be refused approval while the documents are missing (with all three named), upload licence, insurance and four vehicle photos through PG Ride's own store one save at a time exactly as the documents screen does, then the join that failed in the wild — **the driver's own view (`GET /api/driver/profile/me`) and the operator's view (`GET /api/admin/drivers`) must show the same documents for the same record** — then Approve works, approval is what sets `isDriver`, the driver goes online, is offered a ride and completes it for their share. Hiding one document from the operator's view alone fails it, naming which one disagreed.

**One door for texts, in and out (corporate audit #335, 2026-09-26):** every outbound text leaves through `sendSms` in `server/smsService.ts` — the number normalized once, the opt-out registry checked on every send, a Twilio outage never failing the operation. The SMS booking agent (`server/agents/smsBooking.ts`) carried a Twilio client of its own, so a tracking link could be texted to a number that had replied STOP; the emergency test text and the emergency alert built their own clients too. All three now go through the one sender: the test text to a contact who replied STOP is refused with words that say so, and an emergency alert records `smsDeliveryStatus: opted_out` (or `invalid_number`) instead of claiming the text went out — the admins are paged either way. Inbound, both doors are Twilio-signed (`rejectUnlessTwilioSigned`): the legacy `POST /api/sms/inbound` used to book, cancel and issue tracking links on the word of any unsigned POST; it now takes only what Twilio signed, and both doors answer through one function (`answerSignedInboundText`): a STOP-family word records the opt-out and gets no reply, a START-family word from an opted-out number clears it, HELP is Twilio's to answer, and anything else goes to the booking agent and is answered in the same conversation — including YES from a number that is not opted out, which has nothing to clear and is the agent's booking confirmation, not a START (from this PR's own audit; before it the confirm step of an SMS booking was unreachable through the signed door). So one number pointed at one URL gets both the compliance keywords and the booking experience. A number that replied STOP gets no reply to a command at all. The SOS screen tells the rider what happened to the contact's text instead of assuming it went out. Journey 05 covers the forged and unsigned legacy requests, the signed command on both doors, the silence after STOP, and the two emergency texts. Still Festus's to do before texting riders: the Twilio inbound webhook pointed at the signed route, and A2P 10DLC registration (the readiness check keeps saying so).

**A stored file is what its bytes say (corporate audit #340, 2026-09-26):** driver documents and proof-of-delivery photos are reviewed by an operator or a desk in their own signed-in session, so the store never trusts what an upload SAID it was. `shared/uploadTypes.ts` holds the rule: `PUT /api/objects/db-upload/:id` sniffs the bytes (`sniffUploadKind`: JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, HEIC or PDF by signature), stores the file under that type and no other, and refuses anything else (an SVG, an HTML page, a script called .jpg, an empty body) with `UPLOAD_REFUSED_MESSAGE`; the declared Content-Type is only logged. Every stored file, in the database store and in the cloud bucket alike, is served with `safeServeHeaders`: a photo or PDF inline with `nosniff` and `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox`; anything stored before the rule as a download under `application/octet-stream`. The 10 MB cap stays on the raw body parser. The harness uploads real bytes (`tinyPng`, `tinyPdf`); journey 29 refuses a page declared as a PNG, a script declared as a JPEG and an empty body, stores a PNG declared as a JPEG as a PNG, serves a PDF as a PDF and keeps another rider out; journeys 18 and 25 refuse the page and the SVG at the door and prove a legacy page is only ever a download. Cloud uploads go straight to the bucket by signed URL, so their bytes are not checked at the door — serving is where they are held; a malware scan is not in place and is named on the issue.

**Every booking is priced by the server (corporate audit #379, 2026-09-26):** the fare a rider confirms is the fare charged at completion, so the number on the ride is never the app's. The ordinary door had floored the app's number at the server's quote since 2026-09-16; the multi-stop and coworker-group doors still stored whatever `estimatedFare` the app sent, and the multi-stop sheet priced with a tariff nothing else used ($2.50 + $1.50/mi + $0.30/min). Now one rule in `shared/bookingQuote.ts`, applied by `priceBooking` in `server/bookingQuote.ts` at all three doors: the ride's road figures are the app's when they could be true (`roadFiguresPlausible`: no shorter than the straight line, no faster than 70 mph, and — from this PR's own audit — no more than three times the straight line plus a mile and no slower than 4 mph beyond a 20-minute stall, so a one-mile trip cannot be priced to the fare cap on 489 claimed miles), the server's own estimate for the whole route pickup → stops → destination otherwise; the fare is the server's quote for those figures on the live rate card and the requested vehicle class, always. The app's number is compared, not trusted: well under the quote (below 80%) is a tampered app and ops are paged (`fare_mismatch`), well over it (above 125%) is a stale rate card on an old bundle and ops are paged, and in both cases the rider pays the rate card. An app that priced the same figures on the same rate card sends the server's own number, so a rider on a current bundle sees no difference. The coworker organizer's `originalFare` is the server's quote too, so the group rate comes off the right figure. Multi-stop pickups are validated like stops (`validateRoutePoints`). **Statements are issued once and settled once (corporate audit #381 and #382, 2026-09-26):** `issueStatement` does the whole of a week in one transaction with the jobs selected `FOR UPDATE`, so two issuers at once (the Monday sweep and an operator's button, or two weeks both entitled to a late job) cannot both take one job; the stamp carries `statement_id IS NULL` and the statement is written only if every job it priced was stamped by it, so its count and total never disagree with the jobs attached. Collection uses one Stripe idempotency key per attempt (`chargeAttemptKey`, `shared/billingCycle.ts`), with the attempt number stored before Stripe is called: an attempt Stripe never answered (timeout) stays `charging` with no intent id and is repeated under the same key, never a second debit; an attempt Stripe decided against is `failed` and the next one gets a new key, so an operator's retry is a real retry, not a replay of the failure. Settlement believes only the statement's current attempt (`settlementDecision`): a success or failure about a superseded intent is ignored and logged, a settled statement never moves again, and a charging statement with no intent recorded adopts the one Stripe names for it. `payment_intent.processing` and `payment_intent.canceled` reach the statement as well as `succeeded` and `payment_failed`. Journey 17 proves the concurrent issuers and the event correlation with signed webhook events. Hardened after its own audit: the jobs are locked in id order inside a 5-second `lock_timeout`, a deadlock or timeout and the unique (organization, week) index both surface as a 409 that names the other issuer, never a 500. Before any debit is minted for a charging statement with no intent recorded, the organization's recent PaymentIntents on Stripe are searched for one carrying the statement id — a debit that did go through before the process died (or from before attempts were numbered, when the key was a constant) is adopted and never repeated — and if Stripe cannot be asked, nothing is charged and ops are paged; journey 17 covers it. A Stripe idempotency error (the request changed under a reused key, say a bank account swapped mid-retry) ends the attempt with words that say so, and the next attempt is a new key behind that same search.

Now a close only notes the time (`driver_profiles.presence_dropped_at`, the first close starts the clock), a join clears it, and the minute sweep (`releaseRidesOfDroppedDrivers` in `server/routes.ts`; admin `POST /api/admin/analytics/driver-drop-sweep`) releases the driver's claims only once they have been gone for the whole grace (`DRIVER_DROP_GRACE_MS`, 5 minutes, and the window `DRIVER_DROP_WINDOW_MINUTES` in `shared/driverPresence.ts`) and still have no open socket; a stale clock on a driver with a live socket is cleared, not acted on. When it does release a ride the rider is told and the ride offered again as before, and now the driver gets an in-app notification past calm mode naming the pickup and how long they were gone, and ops are paged with the ride. The clock is cleared once acted on, so a driver still gone is paged once, not every minute. Journey 42 opens the driver's own socket and walks the close, the re-join, the raced stale clock and the release.

**A reset clears the lockout and ends other sessions; a failed email is paged and counted (reliability audit, 2026-09-29):** the usual reason a rider resets a password is that they are locked out, and the email reset left the lockout in place, so they were told to wait up to fifteen minutes with the new password in hand; `storage.updatePassword` now clears the counters as the text and admin resets already did. Every reset (by link, by text code, by an admin) ends every session the account holds (`storage.endAllSessionsFor`, the statement revoke and suspend use), because a password is reset when the old one is forgotten or feared stolen and a session opened with it must not outlive that; and a reset by text or admin voids an emailed link still out there. Every email failure used to be swallowed to `console.error`, so an approval notice or reset link that never left was invisible to the operator: `server/emailService.ts` now reports each final failure through a recorder (`setEmailFailureRecorder`, so the module stays free of the database and Telegram for its unit tests), and the one the server registers (`server/emailFailures.ts`) pages ops once an hour per kind of email and class of reason (not configured, quota, auth, recipient, connection) and writes a `reliability_events` row (`email_failed`) for every failure. In production with no app password, `POST /api/auth/forgot-password` answers 503 "Reset by email is not available right now" as the text door does, instead of "check your email". Journey 41 covers the lockout, the ended session, the voided link and the paging (the harness's SMTP is a closed port, so every send fails on purpose). Still open: the reset token is stored in plain text; Gmail's daily quota is not counted ahead of time; names are interpolated into most templates unescaped.

**Quiet never means uninformed (reliability audit, 2026-09-29; priority 1 after it):** calm ride mode and "minimize notifications" used to silence every push, and no ride event passed the bypass flag, so a rider who asked for quiet learned that their driver had cancelled, or was standing outside, only when they next opened the app. The rule is now in one place, `shared/notificationPolicy.ts`: a notification about a ride the person holds (accepted, arrived, started, completed, cancelled, no-show, reminder), a message from the other party on it, a driver's own work (an offer, a claim to confirm, a claim released, a ride at risk) or money to act on always goes through; a credit, a group forming, an announcement respects the preference. `deliverUserNotification` applies it by type, so no send site has to remember a flag; passing `bypassQuietPreferences` still overrides it either way. The in-app row is written whatever the preference; only the push is decided. Unit tests: `shared/notificationPolicy.test.ts` and `server/notificationService.test.ts` (storage and push stubbed).

**The rider's map never freezes silently (reliability audit, 2026-09-29; priority 2 after it):** the driver's position travelled over the WebSocket only, the rider's screen learned of it over the socket only, the socket had no heartbeat (a half-open one counted as delivered, so the chat push fallback never fired), and the app reconnected on a fixed 3-second beat — so a dropped socket froze the rider's map and let the stored position go stale enough for the T-10 ride-risk page to fire on a driver who was driving. The figures are in `shared/liveLocation.ts`. Now the driver app sends its position over the socket while it is up and `POST /api/driver/location` while it is down (same 5-second throttle), and that route does everything the socket path does (forwards to the rider of every active ride, checks the route); `driver_profiles.location_updated_at` stamps every position write, the ride-risk watch reads it (falling back to `updated_at` for rows from before), and `GET /api/rides/active` carries `driver.id`, `driver.currentLocation` and `driver.locationUpdatedAt`, which the rider's 5-second poll feeds into the map whenever it is newer than what the socket gave. The server pings every socket every 30 seconds and drops one that missed the last ping (`runHeartbeat`; admin `POST /api/admin/analytics/ws-heartbeat` runs a round), and the app backs off 3, 6, 12, 24 then 30 seconds with jitter. Journey 43 posts a position over HTTP with no ride id, reads it back on the rider's active ride, proves a profile edit is not a fresh position, and opens one socket that answers pings and one that does not.

**Email hardening (reliability audit, 2026-09-29; priority 3 after it):** every rider-, driver- and desk-supplied string in an email template is now escaped (`escapeHtml` on names, addresses, phones, vehicle, organization and inviter names, circuit names, the reset and invite links), where before only the announcement and SOS templates were, so a driver's name could land in a rider's receipt as raw markup. The transport is pooled (3 connections, 100 messages each) and an announcement's emails go out one at a time in the background, because firing every recipient at once opened a connection per message and Gmail refuses the burst. A send is retried only when it never got as far as handing the message over (`isRetryableSmtpError`: connection, greeting, TLS or sign-in failures) — a timeout after DATA may mean the server accepted it, and sending again delivered a reset link twice. `EMAIL_REPLY_TO` (optional) sets where a reply lands, since several templates say "reply to this email"; it is shown in `getEmailConfigSummary().replyTo`. The summary also carries `sentToday` (per process, per UTC day) against `dailyLimitHint` (Gmail's 500), and a failure classed `quota` or `auth` pages ops with words that say what to do. The receipt says "the card on file", not the discontinued virtual card. Unit tests in `server/emailService.test.ts` (Nodemailer stubbed): the escaping, the pool, the retry rule, Reply-To and the counter.

**Reset tokens are hashed at rest; the text door reveals nothing (reliability audit, 2026-09-29; priority 4 after it):** the emailed reset token used to be stored in plain text, so a read of the users table was a one-hour takeover of any account with a reset in flight. `server/resetTokens.ts` holds the rule: `storage.setPasswordResetToken` stores only the SHA-256 (as invitation links already did) and `getUserByResetToken` hashes what the rider sent — accepting a plain token from a row written before the change for the hour it lives. Journey 41 checks the shape of what the ask wrote and plants tokens it knows the hash of to walk the link. The reset-by-text door (`POST /api/auth/forgot-password-sms`) answered 503 for an account whose number Twilio could not reach and 200 for an unknown email, an existence oracle; it now gives the same generic line to an unknown email, an account with no phone, a number that replied STOP, and a number Verify could not reach — paging ops in the last two so a person can help — and answers 503 only when Verify itself is not configured, which is not about the account. A one-time reset code is not exempt from STOP (noted in `server/smsService.ts`).

**Small communication gaps closed (reliability audit, 2026-09-29; priority 5 after it):** the server sends why a ride was cancelled and by whom, and both apps dropped it — the rider read "Your ride has been cancelled", the driver "cancelled by the rider" even when support or a failed payment did it; both toasts now say who and why, and the rider's cancellation push carries the reason. A pruned push subscription (410/404) and a push send error used to leave no trace; `server/pushService.ts` now writes a `reliability_events` row for each (`push_pruned`, `push_failed`, with the notification's tag), so the morning review can say how many pushes never arrived. The server kept one socket per user, so a second tab silently orphaned the first; `server/wsFanout.ts` keeps every open socket a person has behind one entry that looks like a socket to the rest of the server (`send` reaches all, `readyState` is OPEN while any is), closing one tab leaves the others live, and a driver's drop-grace clock starts only when the last tab closes. Journey 44 opens two tabs for the rider and for the driver and proves all three; unit tests in `server/wsFanout.test.ts` and `server/pushService.test.ts`.
